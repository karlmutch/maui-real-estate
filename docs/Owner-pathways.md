# Maui Kamaole. A Mature Resort Community and Demonstrated Pathway to Sustainable Homeownership

The report considers whether Maui Kamaole should continue to be recognized as a mature resort condominium community consistent with it historically being identified as condo/hotel accommodations in Hawaiʻi DBEDT’s [2005 Visitor Plant Inventory](https://files.hawaii.gov/dbedt/visitor/visitor-plant/vpi2005.pdf), and [Maui Ordinance 4167](https://www.mauicounty.gov/DocumentCenter/View/96114/Ord-4167) - while keeping in mind the stated objective of expanding sustainable pathways to homeownership and stable long-term residency for Maui residents.

This report also evaluates the observable market and ownership outcomes at Maui Kamaole during the period from January 2019 through July 2026.

It draws on publicly available ownership records, conveyance records, mortgage information, statistical analyses, comparative market data gathered from Maui County public datasets and the State of Hawaii Bureau of Conveyances.

## A Shared Objective

Maui County has identified increasing housing opportunities for Maui residents as an important public policy objective. Expanding opportunities for local residents to establish long-term homes supports families, employers, and community stability on Maui.

This report supports that objective and adopts it as its standard for evaluation.

This reports purpose is to evaluate whether the measurable outcomes observed at Maui Kamaole show that recently enacted ordinances are producing better opportunities for sustainable homeownership and residency than the long-established ownership model affected by those ordinances.

The County's determination of whether Maui Kamaole should continue to be recognized as a resort community suitable for H-3/H-4 zoning can support and not be in conflict with its broader housing objectives. The relevant question is whether preserving this established resort community also preserves sustainable pathways to long-term ownership and residency.

---



## The Central Principle

**Established ownership ecosystems that have demonstrably supported sustainable pathways to homeownership and long-term residency incorporating STVR should be changed only when measurable evidence shows that an alternative framework is producing materially better public outcomes.**

For the purposes of this report, an ownership ecosystem includes more than individual property ownership. It encompasses the practical pathways through which ownership can ultimately result in owner occupied housing, the long-term financial sustainability of owners, the financial health of the condominium association, continued access to conventional mortgage financing, and stable community governance.

Successful public policy should be evaluated through demonstrated outcomes, not assumptions about future market behavior. Sustainable homeownership depends not only on the affordability of individual units, but on preserving the financial conditions that allow financing-dependent purchasers to participate in ownership and that enable condominium communities to remain financially resilient over time.

For Maui Kamaole, the evidence indicates that significant market changes have already occurred, while the ownership outcomes that formed the primary objective of those changes are not yet seen. The report further identifies financial sustainability as an important component of those ownership outcomes because financially healthy condominium communities help preserve broad access to conventional mortgage financing, support long-term ownership, and maintain sustainable pathways to homeownership and residency for future generations.

## Why Maui Kamaole Warrants Consideration

Maui Kamaole differs materially from many apartment-zoned communities considered during the development of recent housing policies.

It is a long-established condominium community with units historically identified in the State’s visitor-plant inventory as condo/hotel accommodations, and it has operated for more than thirty years under a stable regulatory framework.

Throughout that period, Maui Kamaole has demonstrated characteristics including:

- viable long-term ownership;
- widespread use of conventional mortgage financing;
- financially sustainable ownership supported by its long-established resort operating model;
- stable homeowners' association governance;
- gradual transitions from seasonal occupancy to permanent residency for owners;
- local residents maintaining long-term ties to Maui; and
- sustained self supporting ownership within South Maui.

Taken together, these characteristics describe more than the operation of a resort community. They describe an **ownership ecosystem**.

Over more than three decades, the resort operating model has enabled many owners to retain ownership while personal, family, and economic circumstances evolved. For some, ownership initially served as a vacation property, seasonal residence, or future retirement home. For others, it can provide a practical means of maintaining a long-term connection to Maui while careers, military service, education, or family responsibilities required temporary relocation elsewhere. In many cases, permanent residency becomes possible only after years of sustained STVR ownership.

These characteristics represent demonstrated outcomes rather than theoretical expectations. They provide context when evaluating whether changing Maui Kamaole's established role as a resort is likely to produce superior housing outcomes.

Later sections examine the financial resilience of this ownership ecosystem—including association health and access to conventional financing—and evaluate whether the current policy framework has produced stronger pathways to sustainable homeownership and residency.

---



## Principal Findings

The available evidence shows a clear distinction between market responses and ownership outcomes during the evaluation period.

**Market responses were measurable.** Maui Kamaole underperformed the comparable South Maui communities by approximately 5.4 percent after the policy announcement, and monthly ownership transfers declined significantly (Mann–Whitney *p* ≈ 0.002). These results indicate weaker relative values and reduced market liquidity.

Lower observed values can improve purchasing conditions only if owners are willing to sell at revised market-clearing prices. The decline in completed transfers is consistent with a stalled market in which some owners may defer a sale or continue to seek prices above current buyer expectations rather than accept discounted values. Reduced liquidity can therefore offset—or negate—the practical effect of weaker values on ownership opportunity: fewer units actually transact and become available to prospective purchasers, including Maui residents.

**Ownership outcomes did not show a comparable change.** Overall ownership remained predominantly associated with non-Hawaii mailing addresses. Among the ten units transferred through mid-July 2026, approximately 80 percent were acquired by purchasers associated with non-Hawaii addresses. The available data do not show a measurable increase in Hawaii-associated ownership.

**The market responded measurably. The intended ownership transition has not yet become evident.** The detailed market, ownership, and financing evidence supporting this conclusion appears in the later sections and appendices.

## Sustainable Pathways to Homeownership and Residency

Maui Kamaole has historically supported several pathways through which owners may ultimately become permanent Maui residents. These pathways often develop gradually rather than through immediate owner-occupancy.

They include:

- long-term ownership;
- gradual transition from seasonal occupancy to residency;
- returning Hawaii residents and Pasifika establishing future homes on Maui;
- retirement or other life-stage transitions;
- conventional mortgage financing; and
- lawful resort operations that can support ownership during transitional years.

The report does not suggest that every owner follows these pathways. It identifies them as demonstrated features of Maui Kamaole's long-established ownership model.

The following section examines one of these pathways in detail: the financial resilience and financing conditions that can allow long-term ownership to be retained and remain accessible to future purchasers.

## Financial Sustainability as a Pathway to Homeownership and Residency

Financial sustainability and conventional financing are two connected components of the ownership pathways identified above.

Ownership accessibility depends not only on market prices or an individual purchaser's finances, but also on the financial health of the condominium project. The mortgage analysis undertaken for this report indicates that conventional financing has historically been an important component of ownership at Maui Kamaole.

For many prospective homebuyers, conventional mortgage financing is available through lenders that ultimately sell eligible loans into the secondary mortgage market through government-sponsored enterprises such as **Fannie Mae** and **Freddie Mac**. These organizations publish condominium project eligibility requirements that extend beyond the qualifications of an individual borrower. In addition to evaluating the purchaser's creditworthiness, lenders assess the financial condition, governance, and delinquency rates of the condominium project to determine whether it remains eligible for financing.

The purpose of these standards is straightforward. A financially healthy condominium association presents lower long-term lending risk, helping preserve both the value and marketability of individual homes while supporting continued access to conventional mortgage financing.

Common project-level considerations include:

- adequate master property and liability insurance;
- sufficient reserve funding for long-term maintenance and capital repairs;
- low levels of delinquent homeowners' association assessments;
- absence of significant deferred maintenance or unresolved structural concerns;
- financially sound association governance; and
- compliance with published condominium project eligibility standards.

Consequently, sustainable pathways to homeownership depend not only upon accessible financing with purchase prices, but also upon maintaining a financially resilient condominium community.

If a condominium project no longer satisfies these standards, or lenders determine that applicable project eligibility requirements are no longer met, prospective purchasers lose access to conventional conforming mortgage products and are instead limited to portfolio lending, non-conforming financing, substantially larger down payments, or cash purchases. The practical effect may be a smaller pool of qualified purchasers and reduced accessibility for financing-dependent buyers.

### Financial Sustainability Supports Sustainable Homeownership

One of the defining characteristics of Maui Kamaole's long-established ownership model has been its ability to support **financially sustainable long-term ownership**. Long-term ownership is sustainable only when owners are able to retain their properties over extended periods.

For many owners, lawful visitor accommodation activity has historically provided one mechanism that helped offset the ongoing costs of ownership during years when permanent residency was not yet practical. This financial flexibility enabled owners to retain their properties while employment, education, military service, family responsibilities, or other life circumstances delayed their return to, or full-time residency on Maui.

This economic function should not be viewed solely as visitor activity.

Ownership costs commonly include:

- mortgage payments;
- homeowners' association assessments;
- insurance premiums;
- maintenance expenses;
- capital improvements; and
- long-term reserve funding.

This report does not conclude that visitor accommodation rights are themselves a prerequisite for successful ownership. Rather, it identifies an important policy consideration.

When evaluating changes affecting mature resort communities, policymakers should consider whether those changes preserve the long-term financial resilience that enables owners to maintain a continuing connection to Maui while progressing toward owner-occupancy or renewed permanent residency. For many households, sustained ownership is an important step in a longer journey that ultimately results in rejoining, or becoming part of the Maui community.

Owners who are able to sustain long-term ownership also contribute to financially resilient condominium communities through the continued payment of assessments, ongoing investment in maintenance, adequate reserve funding, and support for appropriate insurance coverage. These characteristics help maintain the financial stability of the community while preserving sustainable pathways to homeownership and residency for future generations.

### Financing Accessibility and Sustainable Homeownership

One of the County's principal housing objectives is to expand opportunities for Maui residents to become homeowners.

Achieving that objective depends not only upon housing prices, but also upon maintaining broad access to conventional mortgage financing.

For many households, conventional financing provides the practical means through which long-term ownership first becomes attainable. It allows purchasers who intend to establish or maintain a lasting connection to Maui to participate in the housing market without relying exclusively on cash resources.

Financially resilient condominium communities also play an important role in maintaining this accessibility. Strong association governance, appropriate insurance coverage, adequate reserve funding, and responsible financial management are among the characteristics considered in determining whether conventional conforming mortgage financing remains broadly available within a condominium project.

Other Hawaiʻi condominium projects illustrate why project-level financial resilience warrants attention. An illustrative comparison involving reserve funding and association costs appears in **Appendix B**.

If access to conforming financing becomes increasingly limited, ownership opportunities will favor purchasers capable of completing transactions without conventional financing. Such a shift could narrow opportunities for financing-dependent buyers, including many households seeking to establish long-term ownership and, over time, permanent residency.

The evidence presented in this report does not demonstrate that a transition has begun at Maui Kamaole, nor does it conclude that changes to the resort operating model would produce this outcome.

The report does, however, identify an important policy consideration.

Housing policy should be evaluated not only by whether it influences market prices, but also by whether it preserves the financial conditions that support broad access to conventional financing, and ultimately stable ownership. Financially resilient owners, financially healthy condominium associations, stable governance, appropriate insurance, and adequate reserve funding collectively support an ownership ecosystem that enables financing-dependent purchasers to participate in long-term homeownership.

Financing accessibility is a public policy question because it influences who can establish and maintain long-term ownership on Maui and, ultimately, who can become and remain a permanent member of the Maui community.

## A Collaborative Path Forward

The objectives of preserving Maui Kamaole as a mature resort community and increasing housing opportunities for Maui residents should not be viewed as competing goals.

The evidence suggests they can coexist.

Maui Kamaole contributes through employment, visitor accommodations, County revenues, private investment, and demonstrated pathways through which owners ultimately become permanent Maui residents.

Housing policy should seek to expand successful pathways to residency while preserving those that have already demonstrated long-term public value.

Continued measurement, transparency, and periodic review provide a constructive path forward that allows policy to evolve as new evidence becomes available. Maintaining Maui Kamaole status as a legally operated resort contributes to, and supports this objective.

---



## Conclusions

This report has evaluated Maui Kamaole using publicly available ownership records, transaction data, mortgage information, and statistical analysis covering the period from January 2019 through July 2026. Analysis was also done commencing from the original Bill 9 announcement through to mid 2026.

The evidence demonstrates measurable market responses, including reduced relative values, lower market liquidity, and reduced ownership turnover.

The same evidence does not demonstrate comparable changes in ownership outcomes.

Maui Kamaole has demonstrated sustainable pathways to owner-occupancy and stable residency over more than three decades.

The replacement framework has demonstrated measurable market change but has not demonstrated measurably stronger ownership outcomes.

Accordingly, the evidence currently supports preserving Maui Kamaole's established resort community designation through H3/H4 zoning while continuing to evaluate housing policy using measurable ownership outcomes rather than market indicators alone.

This recommendation rests on the principle that established public benefits should be replaced only when measurable evidence demonstrates that an alternative framework produces materially better outcomes.

**Continue measuring outcomes, preserve demonstrated pathways, and reconsider long-established regulatory frameworks only when measurable evidence demonstrates materially superior public results.**

## Appendix A - Evidence and Evaluation

### The Ownership Transition Framework

The evaluation undertaken for this report views sustainable owner-occupancy and residency as the final stage of a sequence rather than the immediate consequence of a policy change.

```text
County Housing Policy
          │
          ▼
Changes in Economic Incentives
          │
          ▼
Changes in Market Conditions
          │
          ▼
Ownership Opportunities
          │
          ▼
Sustainable Owner-Occupancy
          │
          ▼
Long-Term Residency
```

Each stage can be measured independently.

The early stages describe how markets respond.

The later stages evaluate whether those market responses ultimately result in greater opportunities for Maui residents.

This distinction allows policy effectiveness to be evaluated objectively over time.

---



### Necessary Conditions and Sufficient Conditions

The empirical evidence developed during this evaluation indicates that changes in market conditions alone should not be interpreted as evidence that housing outcomes have improved.

Reduced property values may create more favorable purchasing conditions.

They do not necessarily result in conditions that are more favorable to increased owner-occupancy or long-term residency.

In economic terms, changes in market conditions may represent a **necessary condition** for future ownership change without being a **sufficient condition** to produce that outcome.

Additional factors—including financing availability, household income, ownership turnover, purchasing preferences, inventory availability, and broader economic conditions—may also influence whether ownership patterns ultimately change.

Incentivizing certain styles of ownership using taxes accompanying the recent policy changes also shows no material change in ownership.

This recognizes the limits of the available evidence while remaining consistent with the statistical findings in later sections.

---



### Observed Market Responses

This section examines the observable market responses during the implementation period using publicly available transaction records and statistical analyses covering January 2019 through July 2026.

Residential real estate markets are influenced by many factors, including interest rates, insurance costs, economic conditions, construction costs, and local housing demand. The analysis does not attribute every observed market change to a single cause.

Instead, the objective is to identify measurable market responses and determine whether those responses are consistent with the policy outcomes that were anticipated.

#### Data Sources

The market evaluation draws upon multiple independent sources of information, including:

- Maui County conveyance records;
- arm's-length sales transactions;
- repeat-sales analysis;
- event-study analysis;
- counterfactual comparisons with two comparable South Maui condominium communities;
- ownership transfer records; and
- mortgage recording information.

Together these datasets provide a comprehensive view of how the Maui Kamaole market evolved during the evaluation period.

Using multiple independent sources reduces reliance upon any individual dataset and allows findings to be compared across different analytical methods.

---



#### Relative Property Values

Across the study period, Maui Kamaole experienced measurable price adjustments that differed from those observed in comparable South Maui communities.

The counterfactual analysis comparing Maui Kamaole with Wailea Palms, and Palms at Wailea estimated a relative, to the two comparable South Maui communities, decline of approximately **5.4 percent** following the Bill 9 policy announcement.

It measures Maui Kamaole's performance relative to similar communities, not changes in the broader Maui housing market.

The evidence therefore indicates that market participants incorporated changing expectations into property valuations.

This finding demonstrates a measurable market response.

It does not demonstrate an improvement in housing opportunity.

---



#### Market Liquidity

The strongest statistical finding emerging from the evaluation concerns market liquidity.

Transaction activity declined noticeably during the legislative Bill 9 period and subsequent implementation period of the resulting ordinance(s).

Monthly ownership transfers fell significantly compared with earlier years.

Statistical testing using the Mann–Whitney methodology produced a probability value of approximately **p = 0.002**, indicating that the reduction in transaction activity is unlikely to be explained by ordinary month-to-month variation alone.

By mid-2026, approximately ten units had changed ownership for the 2026 year first half, representing annualized turnover of roughly **3.2 percent** of the community.

Fewer completed transfers mean that fewer units changed hands during the period. The implications of reduced turnover for ownership opportunity are examined in **Turnover and Ownership Opportunity** below.

---



#### Market Expectations

Changes in property values and transaction activity together suggest that market participants adjusted their expectations regarding the future economic value of ownership.

These adjustments are consistent with a market responding to increased regulatory uncertainty and changing expectations concerning future use.

#### What the Market Evidence Demonstrates

During the implementation period, the market experienced:

- measurable changes in relative property values;
- statistically significant reductions in market liquidity;
- changing market expectations; and
- divergence from comparable South Maui communities.

These findings show market response, not improved housing outcomes. Changes in market conditions represent intermediate responses.

The County's stated objective is improved opportunities for Maui residents to establish long-term homes. The next section therefore evaluates the evidence that matters most to that objective:

**Did ownership patterns change?**

### Observed Ownership Responses

Unlike property values or recorded sales prices, residency cannot be observed directly through publicly available property records.

Accordingly, this report evaluates ownership using observable indicators including:

- mailing-address records;
- ownership transfers;
- recorded conveyance documents;
- mortgage information; and
- ownership composition over time.

These measures do provide objective indicators that allow ownership trends to be evaluated consistently over time but are limited to what these observable data can reasonably support.

#### Overall Ownership Composition

One of the principal findings of this evaluation is the stability of Maui Kamaole's ownership profile.

Throughout the study period, ownership remained predominantly associated with non-Hawaii mailing addresses.

Current ownership records indicate approximately:

- **84.65 percent** of ownership associated with non-Hawaii mailing addresses; and
- **15.03 percent** associated with Hawaii mailing addresses.

Although individual properties changed hands during the evaluation period, the overall ownership profile changed very little. Measurable market responses did not coincide with a measurable shift in overall ownership composition.

#### Recent Ownership Transfers

The most recent transaction data provide an additional opportunity to evaluate ownership change.

During 2026, ten Maui Kamaole units changed ownership through mid-July.

These transactions represent the newest ownership opportunities created within the community.

Analysis of these transfers indicates that approximately **80 percent** of purchasers were associated with non-Hawaii mailing addresses.

Although the number of transactions is relatively small, these sales provide the earliest observable evidence of how newly available ownership opportunities are being allocated.

After analysis they do not demonstrate any increase in Hawaii-associated ownership.

#### Turnover and Ownership Opportunity

An important relationship emerges when ownership data are considered alongside the market analysis presented in the previous chapter.

Ownership opportunities arise only when properties become available for purchase.

The statistical analysis demonstrated that ownership turnover declined significantly during the evaluation period.

As turnover declines, the number of opportunities for ownership composition to change also declines.

Even if the proportion of Hawaii purchasers were eventually to increase, a market generating fewer ownership opportunities will necessarily change ownership composition more slowly than one with greater turnover.

Consequently, reduced market liquidity may influence the speed at which ownership objectives can be achieved.

#### Financing Accessibility

Mortgage information provides another perspective on ownership opportunity.

Historically, Maui Kamaole ownership has depended substantially upon conventional financing.

The community has not functioned exclusively as a cash market.

However, analysis of the most recent transactions suggests that a relatively large proportion of recent purchases did not have a recorded mortgage at the time of conveyance.

For the purposes of this report, transactions without a recorded mortgage are treated as cash purchases unless evidence indicates otherwise.

This observation does not establish why purchasers elected to complete transactions without recorded financing.

Possible explanations include:

- cash purchases by individual buyers;
- purchases by investment entities;
- financing arranged outside the period covered by available records;
- delayed recording of mortgage instruments; or
- other lawful transaction structures.

Regardless of the explanation, these observations suggest that financing accessibility should remain an important area for continued monitoring because practical access to conventional financing has historically been one of Maui Kamaole's demonstrated pathways to ownership.

#### What the Ownership Evidence Demonstrates

The ownership evidence shows three patterns. First, overall ownership composition remained substantially stable throughout the evaluation period.

Second, the most recent ownership transfers did not demonstrate a measurable increase in Hawaii-associated ownership.

Finally, recent transaction characteristics suggest that financing accessibility warrants continued evaluation because financing has historically represented an important pathway into ownership.

These observations should not be interpreted as evidence that future ownership outcomes cannot change.

Rather, they indicate that the ownership outcomes anticipated by recent housing policy have not yet become measurable within Maui Kamaole.

### Interpreting the Evidence

#### Leading and Outcome Indicators

Housing policy should be evaluated using indicators that reflect both market response and long-term public outcomes.

These indicators serve different purposes.

##### Leading Indicators

Leading indicators measure how markets respond to policy changes.

Examples include:

- changes in relative property values;
- changes in market liquidity;
- ownership turnover;
- transaction volume;
- financing conditions; and
- purchaser expectations.

These indicators often respond quickly following policy changes.

They demonstrate that markets have reacted.

They do not demonstrate that housing outcomes have improved.

##### Outcome Indicators

Outcome indicators measure whether the public objectives of the policy have been achieved.

For Maui Kamaole, these include:

- increases in Hawaii-associated ownership;
- greater participation by Hawaii purchasers in newly available ownership opportunities;
- sustainable owner-occupancy;
- long-term residency; and
- preservation or expansion of practical pathways to homeownership and residency.

Outcome indicators typically develop more slowly than market indicators.

They therefore provide the more appropriate basis for evaluating long-term policy effectiveness.

#### What the Evidence Demonstrates

The evidence presented throughout this report supports several conclusions.

- Property values diverged from comparable communities.
- Market liquidity and ownership turnover declined.

These changes are supported by multiple independent analytical methods.

Ownership records indicate relatively little change in the overall ownership profile of Maui Kamaole.

Recent ownership transfers also continued to be associated predominantly with non-Hawaii mailing addresses.

Together, these results show measurable market change without a measurable change in the ownership profile of the community. Reduced turnover also limited the number of new ownership opportunities; its implications are discussed in **Turnover and Ownership Opportunity** above.

#### What the Evidence Suggests

The evidence also supports several observations that should be interpreted more cautiously.

These are consistent with the observed data but should not be interpreted as proven causal relationships.

- Market change alone is not sufficient

This observation suggests that changing market conditions alone may not be sufficient to produce measurable changes in ownership outcomes.

Other factors—including financing availability, household resources, inventory, purchaser preferences, and broader economic conditions—may also influence ownership decisions.

The available data do not allow these factors to be isolated individually.

- Sustainable pathways should be evaluated as complete systems

Ownership is not created solely through purchase price.

It depends upon multiple interconnected factors, including:

- financing;
- ownership stability;
- the ability to retain ownership;
- gradual transition toward residency;
- and long-term economic sustainability.

Changes affecting any one component may influence the effectiveness of the entire pathway.

Consequently, evaluating only one market indicator may provide an incomplete picture of long-term housing opportunity.

- Financing deserves continued attention

The mortgage analysis suggests that financing accessibility remains an important component of Maui Kamaole's ownership model.

Recent transactions also indicate that a substantial proportion of purchases were completed without a recorded mortgage at the time of conveyance.

The available evidence does not establish why.

However, because conventional financing has historically represented one of Maui Kamaole's principal ownership pathways, future evaluations should continue to monitor whether financing accessibility changes over time.

If financing-dependent purchasers face increasing barriers while cash transactions become relatively more common, this could affect who is able to participate in future ownership opportunities.

This should be viewed as a policy consideration requiring continued observation rather than as a demonstrated conclusion.

#### What the Evidence Does Not Demonstrate

Objective policy evaluation requires acknowledging the limits of available evidence.

This report does not demonstrate:

- that future ownership outcomes cannot improve;
- that future Hawaii resident ownership will not increase;
- that affordability has improved or deteriorated for individual households;
- that any single factor explains the observed ownership patterns.

These questions require additional years of ownership data before definitive conclusions can be reached. These limitations bound what the conclusions can claim.

## Appendix B — Illustrative Project-Level Financing Context: Harbor Lights

Harbor Lights provides an illustrative Hawaiʻi example of how project-level financial conditions may affect financing accessibility and ownership opportunity. It is not presented as evidence that Maui Kamaole has the same conditions or will experience the same outcomes.

Public materials concerning Harbor Lights describe reserve-replenishment efforts and significant association-cost increases. A November 2023 [interview with a Harbor Lights board member](https://www.hoaleader.com/public/Think-You-Cant-Cut-Costs-These-Condos-HOAs-Did.cfm) discussed the project’s financial challenges. Listing disclosures for [Unit D-425](https://www.hawaii.house/condo/harbor-lights/unit-d425/kahului-hi-96732-1249/400738/) and [Unit D-402](https://www.hawaii.house/condo/harbor-lights/unit-d402/kahului-hi-96732/393821/) also identify association fees and reserve-related assessments.

The example illustrates why reserve funding, insurance, maintenance, assessments, and association governance are relevant to project-level financing conditions.
